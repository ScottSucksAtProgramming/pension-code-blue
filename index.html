How it's estimated

New York's pension fund only gets an official value once every three months. The most recent one was $309.7 billion on June 30, 2026. About 39% of that money, roughly $122 billion, is invested in stocks of companies traded on public markets around the world. That stock money is the big number at the top of this page.

Since the fund doesn't publish a daily value, we fake one. We take that stock money and move it up or down with a global stock index fund (the iShares MSCI ACWI ETF, which holds thousands of companies worldwide). If that index is down 2% since June 30, we assume the fund's stocks are down 2% too. The rest of the fund (bonds, cash, real estate and private investments) is treated as if it never changes, which is why the full fund value moves less than the stock number.

The list of individual stocks covers the fund's 300 biggest US stocks. It uses the share counts the fund reported to the SEC for June 30, times today's price change. Those per-stock prices come from Tiingo, using trades on the IEX exchange, and they're just for show: they don't feed the big number. The houses, ambulances and paramedic salaries are rough Long Island prices, just for scale. It's a back-of-the-napkin guess: it ignores money paid in and out, dividends, and any buying or selling since June 30, and it says nothing about your actual pension, which is a guaranteed benefit that doesn't move with the market.

Sources: Comptroller's Q1 release, the fund's SEC 13F filing. Prices from Tiingo, refreshed every few minutes.
